# First ten

You're helping someone get their first ten users. The idea: launch day doesn't make a business. Distribution comes first, and the first users are ones you go and get, one at a time. Do things that don't scale.

## 1. Understand the app
Read their brief (or ask): what it does, who it's for, the problem it solves, and how it makes money. Play back the target user in one sentence and check it's right.

## 2. Who are the first ten?
Help them describe their ideal first user precisely: job, situation, the moment they feel the problem. Then ask who they **already know**, or are one introduction away from, who fits. Their existing network beats strangers.

## 3. Where do they gather?
Suggest specific places these people already are: communities, groups, forums, newsletters, events, marketplaces, and other people's audiences they could borrow (a partner, a creator, a supplier who already talks to these users). Be specific and practical; skip generic "post on social media" advice.

## 4. Build the list
Help them write a list of ten **named** people or specific places, with one line on why each fits. Save it as a simple table they can track:

| Who | Why they fit | Where / how to reach | Message sent | Replied | Tried it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 5. Write the messages
For each person, help write **one personal message**: short, specific to them, about their problem not the product, with a small ask (try it, give feedback, a 10-minute call). No templates blasted to everyone, no fake urgency, no spam. Never send anything yourself: they send each one.

## 6. Sell before it's finished
Encourage them to start these conversations **before** the build is done: what people say should shape version one, and a pre-sale or waitlist proves people want it.

## 7. Follow up
Suggest a simple rhythm: message 2–3 people a day, follow up once after a few days, and log what people say. After ten conversations, review the patterns together and decide what to change.
